Original illustration in the manner of fine-art printmaking: black ink line work on warm cream textured paper. Strong chiaroscuro with deep blacks and bright paper whites; all shading is built from hatching and cross-hatching lines, never painted fills or smooth gradients. Exactly ONE accent color: a flat vermilion (orange-red, like screen-print ink), used only on the single thing that pulls attention in the scene; everything else stays black ink on cream paper. Adults have realistic proportions; faces are partly hidden in shadow, cropped or turned away. Calm, slightly uncanny stillness: tension comes from light, shadow and composition, never horror or gore, and the eater is never shown as guilty, ugly or sinful. Any visible setting is an ordinary modern Korean home. Hatching treatment: copperplate etching / steel engraving. Very fine, dense parallel lines and cross-hatching that follow the form; even large planes such as walls, tablecloth, sky and background are filled with fine parallel line tones. Crisp, meticulous, highly detailed. No background scene: the subject sits alone on plain cream textured paper with generous empty paper around it, like a printed specimen plate, with a soft hatched shadow beneath it. Subject: a cutaway cross-section diagram, like an engraved scientific plate. A soup bowl on a small section of table is cut in half to show its inside; hidden under the table, a soup reservoir connects through a thin tube up into the bottom of the bowl, quietly refilling it so the bowl never empties. A spoon rests in the bowl. No people. The soup in the bowl, tube and reservoir is the only vermilion. No text, letters, numbers, logos, labels or brand marks anywhere; packaging and papers show only blank shapes.
