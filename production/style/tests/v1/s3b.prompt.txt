Original illustration in the manner of fine-art printmaking: black ink line work on warm cream textured paper. Strong chiaroscuro with deep blacks and bright paper whites; all shading is built from hatching and cross-hatching lines, never painted fills or smooth gradients. Exactly ONE accent color: a flat vermilion (orange-red, like screen-print ink), used only on the single thing that pulls attention in the scene; everything else stays black ink on cream paper. Adults have realistic proportions; faces are partly hidden in shadow, cropped or turned away. Calm, slightly uncanny stillness: tension comes from light, shadow and composition, never horror or gore, and the eater is never shown as guilty, ugly or sinful. Any visible setting is an ordinary modern Korean home. Hatching treatment: woodcut / wood engraving. Large solid black areas with white lines carved out of them, bold gouged strokes, fewer but thicker lines, very high contrast, slightly rough hand-carved edges. No background scene: the subject sits alone on plain cream textured paper with generous empty paper around it, like a printed specimen plate, with a soft hatched shadow beneath it. Subject: a symbolic still life of three objects in a row with balanced spacing, like an engraved emblem plate: (1) a simplified anatomical stomach in the manner of an old medical engraving, calm and not gory, standing for the body's signal; (2) a large heaped bowl of popcorn, standing for the amount in front of you; (3) a small plate holding exactly one neat portion, with a single small round dot pressed into its rim like a full stop, standing for an amount decided in advance. No labels. Only the popcorn heap is vermilion. No text, letters, numbers, logos, labels or brand marks anywhere; packaging and papers show only blank shapes.
