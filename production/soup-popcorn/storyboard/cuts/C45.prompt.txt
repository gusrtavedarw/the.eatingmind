Original illustration in the manner of fine-art printmaking: neutral black ink (true black, never brown or sepia) printed on pale cream paper. Strong chiaroscuro with deep blacks and bright paper whites; all shading is built from hatching and cross-hatching lines, never painted fills or smooth gradients. Exactly ONE accent color: a matte red-orange printing ink (vermilion, around #D2452B), opaque and flat like screen-print ink; never pink, never salmon, never brown, never neon. It is used only on the single thing that pulls attention in the scene; everything else stays black ink on cream paper. Adults have realistic proportions; faces are partly hidden in shadow, cropped or turned away. Calm, slightly uncanny stillness: tension comes from light, shadow and composition, never horror or gore, and the eater is never shown as guilty, ugly or sinful. Any visible setting is an ordinary modern Korean home. Paper texture over the WHOLE frame: the image looks printed on real handmade paper, with fine paper grain and faint fibers visible everywhere, including through the black ink areas and the vermilion, with slight ink unevenness where the ink meets the paper. Hatching treatment: copperplate etching / steel engraving. Very fine, dense parallel lines and cross-hatching that follow the form; even large planes such as walls, tablecloth, sky and background are filled with fine parallel line tones. Crisp, meticulous, highly detailed. Shadows are deep and wide: build broad, strong black areas from very dense cross-hatching so the darks read boldly. No background scene: the subject sits alone on plain cream paper with generous empty paper around it, like a printed specimen plate, with a soft hatched shadow beneath it.
